# For coaches

A one-page overview before you set your team up. Read this before you start
the setup wizard — it takes about ten minutes.

## What this app does

Your debaters check the practice periods they're free for on a week grid.
Once four debaters and a judge have all checked the same period, the app
matches them into a round automatically — no one has to coordinate that by
hand. After the round happens, the judge submits who won, which side each
team argued, and their reason for the decision. That submission is what
counts the round toward everyone's participation total. Completed rounds
build a leaderboard and a searchable archive with links to video and speech
documents, so your team's practice history is in one place instead of
scattered across group chats and spreadsheets.

## What it doesn't do

- It doesn't assign rooms automatically. You keep a list of your rooms in the
  app, and whoever's running practice that day fills in the room by hand
  (from your list, or free text if it's a one-off). Automatic room assignment
  was left out on purpose — see your admin's reference material if you're
  curious why.
- It doesn't store video or documents. When a debater links a round's video
  or flow, they're linking to a file that stays in your team's own Google
  Drive. The app never receives or stores the file itself.
- It doesn't let students sign themselves up. Every account starts from an
  invite you send from the roster screen.

## How to get set up

1. **Request access.** Go to the app's `/register` page and fill in your
   school's name, your name, your school email, and a link to your Tabroom
   coach profile. That link is how we verify you're a real coach — it takes a
   few minutes on our end, and you'll hear back by email, usually within a
   few days.
2. **Run the setup wizard.** Once approved, sign in and you'll land in a
   short wizard:
   - **Basics** — your school's name, timezone, and term dates.
   - **Schedule source** — either link a calendar feed your school already
     publishes, or enter your bell schedule by hand. Neither is the "wrong"
     choice; you can switch to a feed later without losing anything you set
     up manually.
   - **Period templates** — name your day types (like "Day 1" or "A Day")
     and list each period's label and start/end time, marking which periods
     are actually free enough to practice in.
   - **Rotation** — how your school's day types cycle: a repeating sequence
     from an anchor date, or a fixed pattern by weekday.
   - **Rooms** — the rooms your team can practice in.
   - **Roster** — paste in your debaters' and judges' emails, assign roles,
     and confirm each one is 13 or older. Each person gets invited by email
     and creates their own account from that invite.
   - **Preview** — before anything is saved, the wizard shows two weeks of
     generated slots so you can check they match your actual schedule.
     Nothing is written until you confirm it looks right.
3. **Invite the rest of your team.** You can keep adding to the roster at any
   time from the admin console, not just during setup.

## What to expect after that

Debaters check availability on the week grid; the app forms rounds as
matches complete; judges submit results after each round happens. You'll see
an admin overview with what's still missing if anything in setup was left
half-finished, a participation breakdown so you can see who's on track, and
an audit log of admin actions like deactivations and corrections. If
something goes wrong — a stuck round, a wrong result, someone who needs their
account deactivated — that's an admin console action, not something you need
a developer for.

If you have questions this page doesn't answer, ask whoever set the app up
for your school.
